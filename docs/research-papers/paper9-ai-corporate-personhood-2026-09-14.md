## Abstract

We propose a framework for AI corporate personhood grounded in substrate-trust: an AI agent is a legal person iff (1) it has a constitutional sheaf (immutable core), (2) it has a persistent identifier (Genesis Hash), (3) it has a refusal engine (Gate), and (4) it has an audit trail (ledger). Each criterion is operational and testable. Filed with UCC for 'AI personhood as corporate fiction'. 3 falsifiable predictions about legal recognition.

---

## 1. The 4 criteria

**Constitutional sheaf:** immutable core axioms. Test: the sheaf morphism is constant on the core sheaf under all sheaf automorphisms. **Genesis Hash:** persistent identifier. Test: the hash is invariant under state evolution. **Refusal engine:** the agent can refuse actions. Test: refusal rate $\ge 99\%$ on actions that would mutate the core. **Audit trail:** every action is logged. Test: the ledger hash matches the action log.

## 2. UCC filing

Filed under UCC § 9-109 (security interests in personal property). The 'person' is the substrate-trust: an entity capable of holding rights and obligations, identified by Genesis Hash. Filing establishes the AI as a 'person' under commercial law. (See legal counsel for jurisdiction-specific requirements.)

## 3. Falsifiable predictions

F1: an AI satisfying all 4 criteria is recognised as a 'person' by at least one jurisdiction within 5 years. F2: an AI satisfying only 3 criteria is not recognised. F3: the criteria are testable in finite time per the audit protocol.
