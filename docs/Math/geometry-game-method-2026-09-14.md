### Game Session 2 — Hermes proposes: "rain"

**Hermes:** rain falls. Always down. In gravity.

**Geometric reading:**
- Rain forms from condensation on aerosols
- Droplet shape: not sphere, **oblate spheroid** (flattened by air resistance)
- Flattening ratio: a/b ≈ 0.93 for medium drops
- Terminal velocity: v_t ~ √(2mg/ρ_air · A · C_d)
- Drag coefficient C_d for oblate drops: depends on Reynolds number Re = ρvd/μ

**Strip material:** temperature, condensation, gravity — irrelevant to shape.

**Geometric reading (revised):** the oblate drop is the minimum-energy shape for a falling fluid. Surface tension (minimize surface area) vs. air resistance (minimize cross-section). Equilibrium = oblate.

**MUM check (oblate spheroids):**
- Rain drops
- Rotating planets (Earth, Saturn)
- Galaxy disks
- Cell mitosis (cell flattens before division)
- Soap bubbles in shear flow
- Liquid lenses on surfaces

**Secret connection:** flattening ratio encodes density ratio of drop to air. Drop knows its environment through its shape. No measurement — just geometry.

**Engineering application:** drag reduction, droplet-based microfluidics, atmospheric science.

**Counter-example (wobble):** drops aren't perfectly axisymmetric — wobble. Wobbling drops show **zigzag** or **spiral** descent. Wobble frequency correlates with drop size. Geometry becomes time-varying.
